Teaching & student support

Statistics Support Space

A friendly place to ask questions, strengthen your statistical thinking, and build confidence.

Statistics can feel challenging when a course moves quickly or when a small gap in understanding makes the next topic harder. This space is here to help you work through concepts, ask questions that you may not have had a chance to ask in class, and develop more confidence in statistical reasoning.

A statistical perspective
“Statistics is just an approximation!”

A good approximation is transparent about its assumptions, honest about its uncertainty, and useful for decisions.

Why this space?

Questions are part of learning statistics

Not everyone finds it easy to ask questions during a lecture. The pace may be fast, the room may be large, or you may simply need more time to formulate the question. Language and cultural differences can also make classroom participation more difficult.

My goal is to provide another route for learning: a setting where you can slow down, revisit an idea, and talk through the reasoning behind a statistical method.

Students from all backgrounds are welcome. In particular, I want students who may feel less represented or less comfortable speaking in large STEM classrooms to know that asking for support is encouraged.

How I can help

What we can work on together

01

Concepts & intuition

Revisit definitions, probability ideas, statistical models, assumptions, estimation, testing, regression, and other course concepts that are not yet clicking.

02

Problem-solving strategies

Learn how to identify what a problem is asking, organize the information provided, choose an appropriate statistical approach, and check whether an answer makes sense.

03

Interpretation

Practice moving beyond calculations to explain estimates, uncertainty, confidence intervals, tests, model output, and statistical conclusions in clear language.

04

Study & exam preparation

Discuss ways to review material, diagnose recurring mistakes, organize practice, and prepare efficiently for quizzes, midterms, and final examinations.

A few principles

Support-space guidelines

Respect

Every genuine question is welcome. Discussions should be patient, professional, and respectful of other students.

Participation

Bring your questions and your own attempt when possible. Learning is most effective when we reason through the problem together rather than simply looking for an answer.

Inclusivity

Students from all backgrounds are welcome, including students navigating language, cultural, accessibility, or confidence barriers in the classroom.

Integrity

Assistance must remain consistent with the expectations of your course. When the boundary is unclear, ask before sharing or working on assessed material.

Independence

The goal is not only to solve today's problem, but to leave you better equipped to solve the next one on your own.

Need help?

Start with the question you have.

You do not need to know exactly what is confusing before asking for help. Identifying the difficult part is often the first step.
